# Findings after EXP-052..081 (2026-10-05, /goal "beat v6 at the improvement level, pure LP, time-split holdout")

Goal (Dino, 2026-10-05): a pure Uniswap LP strategy that beats v6 at the **improvement** level — continuous ETH and WBTC CAGR
above v6's, Calmar ≥ v6's, max DD no more than 3 pts deeper, ETH yearly wins ≥ 3/5 — and again on a pre-registered,
**time-split** out-of-time holdout (H5 ETH/USDC 2021-05-06..12-31 and H4 WBTC/USDC 2022-01-01..10-31, both before the dev
windows). Structural changes only, no retries of failed directions, stop at the first pass or after 30 experiments.
Ruling during the goal: idle-capital yield (lending, stablecoin LP of the reserve) is not a strategy change and does not count.

**Result: 30 experiments, no improvement-level pass.** One experiment passed its rule but does not count (v6.39, idle reserve in
a USDC/USDT LP); five passed dev and failed the time-split holdout, each winning one window and losing the other. Every EXP is in
`registry.csv`, Jira QUAN-933..964 (all Done) and the dashboard (`experiments/EXP-052..081`).

## What the series learned

1. **v6 is a range harvester, not a trend follower.** Its edge is the staged refill on rebounds *below* the EMA (EXP-067: refill
   only above the EMA → ETH CAGR 2.1%; EXP-068: plain EMA filter → 2.5%; EXP-079: full ladder on the first stage → 5.7%; EXP-081:
   one-stage refill → 7.8%). The fractional, staged F and the lower stop (EXP-066: without it the May 2021 crash costs −24.6%) are
   load-bearing. The virtual upper rebuild (EXP-069) and the downward-exit rebuild (EXP-075) are inert on this data; the ensemble of
   four spans is worth little either way (EXP-076, EXP-077).
2. **ETH and WBTC disagree about recentring, and the reason is the pools.** Every rebuild-rule change moved the two assets in opposite
   directions. On the 0.05% ETH pool more recentring raises the return (weekly recentre: ETH CAGR 17.1-17.5%; early upside recentre,
   EXP-023: 18.3%); on the 0.3% WBTC pool it halves fee income. Two mechanisms: (a) the inverted-gaussian valley holds most liquidity
   at its ±20% edges, so fees peak when the price has drifted into a thick edge band and a recentre puts it back into the thin centre
   (an extra +10% recentre halved WBTC fees, $16.8k → $8.1k in six months: this closes EXP-023's open question — not a bug); (b) the
   WBTC pool pays its LPs far more per unit of risk (trailing 7-day fee/LVR median 3.49 vs 1.23 on ETH, ≥ 1 on 100% vs 80% of days),
   so time at the thick edge is worth more there. The rebuild swap cost is real (WBTC ~$8.5k of pool fee on $2.8M of swaps, 14% of
   its LP fees; ETH $1.8k, 2%) but it is not the driver: a cost gate on the weekly recentre passed 10 of 11 WBTC checks (not run).
3. **Recentring on F changes is part of v6's ETH edge.** Resizing in place in any form (EXP-055, 058, 061, 062, 072) cost ETH fees;
   new capital placed at the old ticks or in a fresh thin centre earns less than v6's recentred ladder.
4. **The time-split holdout is what separates luck from edge.** The cross-pool holdouts of earlier goals share the dev price paths;
   H5 (ETH 2021 bull with the May crash) and H4 (BTC 2022 bear) do not, and they caught every dev pass: missing crash protection
   (066), more beta in the bear (073), regime-specific timing (063), noise-sized effects (060).
5. **Own-equity overlays fail for an LP.** CPPI (EXP-064) empties the book in a drawdown, and an empty LP book cannot earn its way
   back to the high-water mark (WBTC CAGR 3.3%).

## What remains open

- **Asset-specific versions are possible, a joint one was not found.** For ETH alone, weekly recentre above EMA100 (EXP-071) passes
  every dev condition (CAGR 17.5%, Calmar 0.72, wins 3/5); weekly recentre + share 50% (EXP-070) has Calmar 0.89 with max DD −18.2%.
  For WBTC alone, one-stage refill (EXP-081, Calmar 1.21), no exit rebuild (EXP-074, Calmar 1.18) and resize in place (EXP-055,
  Calmar 1.22) lower risk. None was holdout-tested as an asset-specific strategy; that needs a new goal definition (per-asset
  improvement) and its own pre-registration.
- **Execution, not strategy:** routing WBTC rebuild swaps through the 0.05% WBTC/WETH + WETH/USDC pools would cut ~2/3 of the swap
  bill. Not tested (like idle yield, it changes cost, not the LP rule); ask before counting it.
- **Holdouts are used up.** H4 / H5 have now been run for five variants of this goal and for EXP-040..051 before it; the next clean test of anything is the forward window after
  2026-09-17 (fetch with `samples/fetch_uni_minute.py`; ~3 weeks exist on 2026-10-05).
- Multiple testing: 81 experiments on these two pools since 2026-09; treat any future single dev pass as a candidate for the forward
  window, not as evidence.

## Method notes

- `judge.py oot <tag> <prefix>` judges the time-split holdout; runs need `BINANCE_WARM=1`.
- The shared `~/.demeter` cache crashed a continuous segment in three of eleven batches (pickle EOF / truncated) even with 45-90 s
  launch spacing; each was rerun alone and noted under *Deviations*. Launch the two continuous segments in separate invocations.
- Helper patterns used this goal (not committed): result blocks generated from the run CSVs; registry edits that keep the file's
  mixed CRLF/LF line endings (a naive CSV rewrite changes ~100 lines).
- EXP-016's `resume_ladder` re-adds the (1 − F) reserve to the ladder after a pause (found while building EXP-059); EXP-016's
  verdict should be read with that in mind.
