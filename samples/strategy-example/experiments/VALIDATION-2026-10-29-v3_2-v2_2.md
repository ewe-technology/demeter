### Method and reference rule (pre-registered 2026-10-30, before any yearly number was computed)

Nothing in v3.2 / v2.2 is refitted, so L1 reduces to one yearly table with the constants held fixed, and L5 to
pseudo-OOS on the later years (all pool data predates the 2026-10-27 spec freeze; `VALIDATION-LAYERS-FIT.md`). No new
backtests: the table cuts the six L0 runs above (same engine, costs, windows; data ends 2026-08-31).

Windows, on the UTC-0 daily net value of `strategy_layers.py` (`daily_net_value`):
- calendar years: each year a run covers, from its first daily value in the year (00:00 on 1 January, or the run
  start) to the first daily value of the next year (00:00 on 1 January, or the run's last value). A year the run does
  not cover in full is marked partial (v3.2 2021 from 05-06, v2.2 `0x4585` 2021 from 11-02, `0x7aea` 2024 from 09-14,
  every 2026 row to 08-31).
- anchored windows: from 2022-01-01 to the end of 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 and to the run's end (`0x7aea`: from its
  start, to the end of 2024, 2025 and the run's end).
- per window: return (end / start − 1), CAGR (calendar days), max drawdown inside the window, Calmar = CAGR / |max DD|.
  Return > 0 means beating the run's unit held still (USDC for v3.2, ETH for v2.2).

Reference pass rules, per run (both reinvest modes must pass for the strategy to pass; a reference, not the decision):
- **L1 (yearly stability)**: return > 0 in at least 3 of the 4 full years 2022-2025. The partial years are reported,
  not judged.
- **L5 (pseudo-OOS)**: the later window 2024-01-01..run end has CAGR > 0 and Calmar >= 0.5 x the Calmar of the earlier
  window 2022-01-01..2023-12-31 (if the earlier Calmar is <= 0, CAGR > 0 alone).
- Judged runs: v3.2 on `0x88e6`, v2.2 on `0x4585`. v2.2 on `0x7aea` (2 years, one full year) is reported, not judged.
- Clean OOS (data after the 2026-10-27 freeze): none yet. Rows are added monthly from the first full month (2026-11),
  by refetching the pools and rerunning the frozen code; they do not change the verdict above until Dino says so.
  *Changed 2026-11-03 (Dino): clean OOS is not run. The text above is kept as pre-registered.*

These thresholds are my reading of "judged on the later years" (`VALIDATION-LAYERS-FIT.md` L5); no source fixes them.

Statistics added after the results (2026-10-30, Dino asked for every number plus its theory): on the daily log
returns of the same windows (`l1_l5_stats`), Sharpe (annualised x sqrt(365)) and PSR(0) per full year, over the
whole span from 2022-01-01 (or the run start) and per L5 window, a Sharpe difference test between the L5 windows, and Pardo's walk-forward efficiency. Windows with fewer than
30 daily returns get no statistics.

### Theory

| measure | what it answers | source | conventional bar | limits here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| return > 0 per year (reference L1) | did the strategy beat its unit held still that year | L0 method 1 applied per year | none; 3 of 4 is my choice | 4 years carry little evidence: a coin flip gives 4 of 4 with probability 1/16 = 6%, 3 or more of 4 with 31% |
| Calmar later vs earlier (reference L5) | did return per unit of drawdown hold up | Young (1991) defines Calmar; the 0.5 ratio is mine | none | expected max drawdown grows with window length (Magdon-Ismail & Atiya 2004), so the 2.7-year later window is compared against a 2-year one: biased against the later window |
| PSR(0) | probability that the true Sharpe is above 0, given the sample length, skewness and kurtosis | Bailey & López de Prado (2012); `strategy_validation.py` method 3 | 0.95 (5% one-sided) | assumes independent daily returns; one year is 365 returns, so a yearly Sharpe near 1 cannot reach 0.95 |
| Sharpe difference z, p (later worse) | is the later Sharpe lower than the earlier one beyond sampling noise | variance of the Sharpe estimate under non-normal returns, Mertens (2002), Opdyke (2007) (the same D as PSR); the two windows do not overlap, so their variances add | p < 0.05 | independent returns assumed (no autocorrelation term, Lo 2002): LP daily returns are likely autocorrelated, so the standard error is probably understated and z / p optimistic; Ledoit & Wolf (2008) is the paired test for two series on the same dates, not this case |
| WFE = later CAGR / earlier CAGR | how much of the earlier annual return survives into the later years | Pardo (2008), walk-forward efficiency | about 0.5 or more is the usual rule of thumb (practitioner, not statistical; page not checked) | Pardo compares a refitted model's OOS with its IS fit; nothing is refitted here, so it measures decay only |

### Objective assessment

What the numbers show:
- v3.2 gains in every year in USD (+10% to +26% a full year; 2026 to 08-31 +15.5% / +8.3%). Its later Sharpe (0.88 /
  0.89) is not below the earlier one (0.77 / 0.80; z > 0) and WFE is 1.00 / 0.78: return per unit of volatility held
  up. Its Calmar fell (1.48 to 0.93 / 0.83) because the 2024 drawdown was deeper (−24.3% vs −15.1% with reinvest on).
  A longer window tends to show a deeper max drawdown (theory table); how much of this drop that explains is not
  measured. With reinvest off the Calmar ratio is 0.56, near the 0.5 reference bar.
- v2.2 gains in every year in ETH, but most of its record is 2022 (+186% / +173%), the ETH/BTC fall it sat out in BTC
  (L0 note). Its Sharpe falls from 1.87 / 1.83 to 0.78 / 0.63, WFE is 0.23 / 0.22 (Pardo's rule of thumb is about 0.5; theory table), and the
  2025 drawdown is −36% / −45%. 2023 alone already had a Calmar of ~5, so the drop is not only the 2022 outlier.
- `0x7aea` (v2.2's target pool) shows the same 2025 picture (Sharpe 0.31 / 0.24, drawdown −37% / −40%), so the
  later-years weakness is not specific to mainnet.

What the numbers cannot show:
- Significance per year or per half. Over the whole span 2022..2026-08 both strategies reach PSR 0.95 (v3.2 0.963 /
  0.965, v2.2 0.999 / 0.997; `0x7aea` from 2024-09 does not: 0.882 / 0.841). Inside it, no single v3.2 year and no
  v3.2 L5 window does: one year of daily returns cannot separate a Sharpe near 0.8 from 0 at 95% (it needs about 1.65).
  Of v2.2's windows only 2022, 2023 and the earlier half do. v2.2's Sharpe drop is not significant at 5% (p 0.10 /
  0.085): the data cannot tell a real decay from noise, either way. These PSR and p values assume independent daily
  returns; if LP returns are autocorrelated they are optimistic.
- Generality. One price path per pool; the later years are pseudo-OOS (the spec was frozen after them). Clean OOS is not run (Dino, 2026-11-03); it would start
  2026-11. Gas is not charged (team ruling), and JIT dilution of fees is not modelled (L0).
- A benchmark. Return > 0 beats only the unit held still (USDC, ETH), not holding ETH (v3.2) or BTC (v2.2).

## L6 regime split

Four parts, as L1 + L5: method and reference rule, theory, results, objective assessment.

### Method and reference rule (pre-registered 2026-10-30, before any regime label or result was computed)

Two parts (`VALIDATION-LAYERS-FIT.md` L6): (a) split the six L0 runs by market regime; (b) rerun each pool one step
either side of the two main constants and check whether the best setting changes with the regime.

**Regime rule** (ours; Maker's `pair_regime.py` is not used, ruling 2026-10-29). On a Binance daily close series c
(`binance_<pair>_1d.csv`, from 2017-08-17), judged on day D's close:
- EMA200 = exponential moving average of c, span 200, `adjust=False`, seeded on the first close (2017-08-17);
- slope = EMA200(D) / EMA200(D − 30 days) − 1;
- drawdown = c(D) / max(c over the 365 days up to and including D) − 1;
- **bull**: slope >= +2% and drawdown > −20%. **bear**: slope <= −2% and drawdown <= −20%. **range**: every other day.

The thresholds are fixed here, before any label is computed, and are not tuned afterwards. 20% below the high is the
common convention for a bear market; ±2% per 30 days on EMA200 separates a trending average from a flat one (my
choice, no source).

**Split (a)**: the run's daily log return from 00:00 of day D to 00:00 of day D + 1 (`daily_net_value`) gets the label

### Theory

| measure | what it answers | source | conventional bar | limits here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bull / bear / range labels | does the result depend on one kind of market | rule-based dating of bull and bear markets: Pagan & Sossounov (2003, J. Applied Econometrics 18(1)); 20% moves as bull / bear thresholds: Lunde & Timmermann (2004, JBES 22(3)). Model-based alternative not used: Hamilton (1989) Markov switching | 20% below the high = bear is a convention; the ±2% EMA200 slope is mine | the labels are fixed after the fact (they use day D's close); they describe, they cannot be traded. Our rule adds a slope test, so "range" also holds days the two tests disagree on (below) |
| per-regime annualised return, Sharpe | did it gain in each kind of market, per unit of volatility | as L1 + L5 (Sharpe, PSR) | L6a: > 0 in every regime (mine) | the effective sample is the episodes, not the days: v3.2 has 9 bear and 25 bull episodes, v2.2 `0x4585` 5 bull episodes on ETH/BTC. The standard error of an annual Sharpe from n daily returns is about sqrt(365 / n) (Lo 2002, independent returns): 0.78 for 595 days, 1.24 for 239 days |
| one step either side of each constant | is the spec on a broad plateau or a sharp peak, and does the best neighbour change with the regime | parameter stability: Pardo (2008), *The Evaluation and Optimization of Trading Strategies*, ch. on robustness (neighbouring values should perform similarly; page not checked); overfit risk of a lone peak: Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu (2014) | none; L6b "the same setting best in every regime" is mine | very strict: if the three settings were equal up to noise, one setting wins all three regimes with probability 1/9 per constant (1/81 for both), so a fail says little alone. Three settings per constant do not map a plateau; they test one step. All runs share one price path per pool |

### Objective assessment

What the numbers show:
- v3.2 gains in bull (+46% / +35% a year) and bear (+38% / +26%) markets in USD, and loses in range (−8% / −7.5%).
  The range loss comes from one kind of day: a close 20% or more below its 365-day high while the EMA200 still rises
  (239 days in 24 short episodes, the first weeks of a crash after a rally; ETH fell at −89.5% a year on those days).
  There v3.2 lost −41% / −34% a year. On those days the slow EMAs still point up; how much of the book was deployed
  on them is not measured here. On the flat-EMA range days it gains (+24% / +17%). Every one of the five settings loses on range days (−1% to −12%),
  so this is a property of the design, not of the spec constants.
- v2.2 gains in ETH in every ETH/BTC regime on both pools. On ETH/USD labels it loses in ETH bull markets (−17% to
  −28% a year in ETH while ETH/USD rose +130% to +164%): it holds BTC / USDC through ETH rallies. That is expected for a
  book counted in ETH; it is the same finding as EXP-180 (v2's ETH drawdowns come from holding BTC / USDC through ETH
  rebounds).
- Neighbours: no constant sits on a sharp peak. One step changes the full-run CAGR by at most 4 pts (v3.2 22.6% to
  27.7%, spec 23.7%; v2.2 `0x4585` 42.4% to 46.3%, spec 46.3%). On v3.2 two neighbours do better than the spec on CAGR
  and Calmar (EMA +10, width x1.1); width x0.9 has a 5 pt deeper drawdown. On `0x4585`, the pool the v2.2 constants
  were calibrated on, the spec is the best of the five; on `0x7aea` width x1.1 is better in every regime. Max DD
  barely moves on v2.2 (−36.0% to −36.2%).
- L6b fails for both judged strategies: which neighbour is best changes with the regime. The theory table explains why
  this alone says little: the rule passes by chance only 1 time in 9 per constant even if the settings are equal.

What the numbers cannot show:
- Significance. With 9 to 25 episodes per regime, no per-regime Sharpe is far from noise: v3.2's range Sharpe −0.31
  over 595 days has a standard error near 0.78; its "drop, EMA up" Sharpe −1.83 over 239 days near 1.24 (about 1.5
  standard errors, not significant at 5%; returns assumed independent).
- A regime model. The labels are ours and after the fact; another rule (other thresholds, Markov switching) would cut
  the days differently. The range-part split was added after the main table was seen.
- A plateau. Three settings per constant test one step, not the shape of the surface; the two constants were moved one
  at a time, not together. Every run shares one price path per pool; gas is not charged and JIT dilution is not
  modelled (L0).

## L8 PSR + L4 multiple testing + L3 block bootstrap

Four parts, as L1 + L5: method and reference rule, theory, results, objective assessment. All three layers read the
daily log returns of the six L0 runs (`daily_net_value`, data to 2026-08-31); no new backtest.

### Method and reference rule (pre-registered 2026-10-30, before any L3 / L4 / L8 number was computed)

Sample: per run, the daily log returns r_t = ln(V_t / V_t-1) of the whole run, from its first daily value to its last
(v3.2 `0x88e6` from 2021-05-06, v2.2 `0x4585` from 2021-11-02, `0x7aea` from 2024-09-14). L1 + L5 already gave the
Sharpe and PSR of the span from 2022-01-01; L8 uses the whole run. Judged runs, both reinvest modes: v3.2 `0x88e6`
and v2.2 `0x4585`; `0x7aea` is reported, not judged. v3.2 in USD, v2.2 in ETH.

**L8 (PSR)**, per run:
- Sharpe (daily, annualised x sqrt(365)), skewness, kurtosis, PSR(0) with them (`strategy_validation.py` method 3).
- Minimum track record length MinTRL: the days of returns this Sharpe, skew and kurtosis need for PSR(0) >= 0.95,
  MinTRL = 1 + D^2 x (z_0.95 / SR)^2, with D^2 = 1 − skew x SR + (kurt − 1) / 4 x SR^2 (daily SR); compared with the
  days the run has.
- Autocorrelation check: lag-1 autocorrelation rho1 of r_t and the Ljung-Box Q over lags 1..10 with its p-value. As
  a sensitivity, PSR(0) recomputed with the AR(1) effective sample size T_eff = T x (1 − rho1) / (1 + rho1) in place
  of T (only when rho1 > 0; reported, not judged).
- Reference rule **L8**: PSR(0) >= 0.95 over the whole run.

**L4 (multiple testing)**, per run:
- Trial ledger: every spec version and every parameter setting Maker, Steven or the CTO tried while shaping v3.2 /
  v2.2, counted from the `remix` / `maker-defense` git history and the spec documents in the repo
  (`VALIDATION-LAYERS-FIT.md` open questions). N = the ledger total; anything not recorded there is not counted, so N
  is a lower bound. The ledger is written into the results with each trial's source.
- Deflated Sharpe ratio DSR = PSR(SR0) with SR0 = the expected maximum Sharpe of N trials whose true Sharpe is 0:
  SR0 = sqrt(V) x ((1 − gamma) x Phi^-1(1 − 1/N) + gamma x Phi^-1(1 − 1/(N e))), gamma = 0.5772 (Euler-Mascheroni).
  No daily return series exists for the other trials, so V, the variance of the trials' Sharpe estimates, is taken
  under the null as the variance of one Sharpe estimate, V = 1 / (T − 1) (daily units).
- Also reported: DSR at N = 1, 10, 100, 1,000, and the break-even N (the largest N at which DSR >= 0.95).
- Reference rule **L4**: DSR >= 0.95 at the ledger's N.

**L3 (block bootstrap)**, per run, with `strategy_validation.py`'s stationary bootstrap (Politis & Romano 1994) of
the run's daily log returns, one random stream per run seeded with 14 (`Settings.seed`):
- mean test: one-sided 1 − p that the mean daily log return is > 0, 5,000 draws, mean block 20 days (method 3);
  sensitivity at mean blocks 5 and 60 days (reported, not judged).
- one-year paths: 10,000 paths of 365 days, mean block 90 days (method 5): median and 5th percentile of the path's
  max drawdown and return, and the loss probability (share of paths that end below their start).
- 90-day kill line: 5th percentile of 90-day returns, mean block 20 days (method 4).
- beside them, from the real run: the worst 365-day return (rolling, daily values) and the run's max drawdown.
- Reference rule **L3**: 1 − p >= 0.95 (block 20) and one-year loss probability <= 10% (block 90).

Thresholds: 0.95 is the usual one-sided 5% level (PSR, DSR, the bootstrap 1 − p); the 10% loss probability is my
choice, no source fixes it. All rules are references; the team decides.

| measure | what it answers | source | conventional bar | limits here |

| MinTRL | how many days of returns this Sharpe needs before PSR(0) reaches 0.95 | same paper | days available >= MinTRL | same assumption |

| stationary bootstrap | the mean's sampling distribution, and one-year outcomes, keeping short-run dependence (blocks) | Politis & Romano (1994), JASA 89(428); `strategy_validation.py` methods 3-5 | 1 − p >= 0.95; loss probability <= 10% (mine) | resamples the strategy's own returns on one price path; it cannot show how the LP rule acts on other paths. ## L2 decision test (circular shift)

Four parts, as L1 + L5: method and reference rule, theory, results, objective assessment. This is our substitute, not Maker's
MCPT: his permutes prices, which needs synthetic swap data (`VALIDATION-LAYERS-FIT.md` L2).

### Method and reference rule (pre-registered 2026-10-30, before any shifted run)

Question: on the one real price path, does the strategy's timing beat the same decisions placed at other dates?

**What moves.** The daily decision columns the real book reads, together, by k days around the run's judged days (start − 1 ..
end − 1, as a close is executed the next day): F'(D) = F(D − k), the last k days wrapping to the front (`rotate_decisions`).
- v3.2: `Fp` and `diverged` (F' and the divergence flag).
- v2.2: `F` and `strong` (F and the strength switch).

**What stays real.** Minute prices and pool data, fees, range breaks, v3.2's EMA100 ETH share at a build, v3.2's gas cap, claims,
reinvest, costs. All L0 settings stay (Rust, flat 0.1% swap, fee in-range, v3.2 gas cap).

**Why a shift and not the fit note's "shuffle the dates of F changes" (deviation from `VALIDATION-LAYERS-FIT.md`).** With
`F_BY_VALUE` on, F changes on most days, as it follows the virtual ladder's value, so "the same count of changes on random dates"
would be almost the real series. A shift keeps every value, every spacing and the autocorrelation (except at the one wrap) and
only breaks the alignment with prices. Drawing daily values at random would create daily rebuilds and test cost, not timing.

*Correction 2026-11-02, after the runs (the text above is kept as pre-registered): "F changes on most days" is false and was not measured. See Deviations (L2).*

**Grid (no random numbers).** Every step days from 30 to days − 30 (`L2_GUARD` = 30: a shift closer than 30 days to the real
alignment is nearly the real rule), with the widest step that gives at least 500 shifts (the fit note's 500-1,000):

| run | window | judged days | step | shifts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v3.2 `0x88e6` | 2021-05-06..2026-08-31 | 1944 | 3 | 629 |
| v2.2 `0x4585` | 2021-11-02..2026-08-31 | 1764 | 3 | 569 |
| v2.2 `0x7aea` (reported) | 2024-09-14..2026-08-31 | 717 | 1 | 658 |

Each shift gives both reinvest modes. The real run is the L0 run of the same mode.

**Numbers per run and mode** (`l2_row`, `l2_report`): CAGR and Calmar (L0's `l0` on the daily net value), the shifted runs' 5% /
median / 95% CAGR and median Calmar, p = (1 + shifts at least as good as the real run) / (1 + shifts) for CAGR and for Calmar, the
real and the median shifted rebuild count, the decision series' memory (first lag where the autocorrelation of `Fp` / `F` over the
run is under 1/e) and independent alignments = days / memory.

**Reference rule (a reference, not the decision).** L2 passes when p(CAGR) <= 0.05. Judged per run, both modes: v3.2 `0x88e6`,
v2.2 `0x4585`. `0x7aea` is reported, not judged, as in every layer after L0. p(Calmar) is reported beside it.

The switch changes no number when off: checked against the L0 files before any shift runs.

### Theory

| measure | what it answers | source | conventional bar | limits here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| permutation test of a trading rule | could a rule with no timing skill reach this result by chance | Aronson (2006), *Evidence-Based Technical Analysis*, Wiley (Masters' Monte Carlo permutation: pair the rule's daily outputs with shuffled returns, p = share of noise rules at least as good; chapter and page not checked); Masters (2018), *Permutation and Randomization Tests for Trading System Development* (not read in full) | p <= 0.05 by convention | those shuffle returns; a path-dependent LP cannot replay shuffled returns on swap data, so we move the decisions |
| p = (1 + b) / (1 + m) | the exact p when m permutations are drawn; never 0 | Phipson & Smyth (2010), *Stat. Appl. Genet. Mol. Biol.* 9(1), art. 39, [doi](https://doi.org/10.2202/1544-6115.1585) | | they treat random draws; our grid is fixed, and its neighbours are strongly dependent (below), so p is a rank among alignments |
| circular shift | keeps the series' own dependence, which an i.i.d. shuffle destroys | permutation tests lose exactness on dependent data: Romano & Tirlea (2020), [arXiv:2009.03170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03170) | | known defects, Kalyuzhny (2020), *Global Ecology and Biogeography* 29(6), 1085-1093, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1111/geb.13083): the wrap breaks the autocorrelation at one point, and a shift scrambles the initial state, which raised false positives in that field. Here the wrap adds one artificial decision jump per run, and the shifted run starts from another day's F |
| independent alignments | how many really different tests the grid holds | our own rough count, days / memory, using the 1/e convention for a decorrelation time | | 500+ shifts are not 500+ tests. With few independent alignments, a real p under 0.05 is hard to reach, and a low p is weaker than it looks. The count is a rule of thumb, not a derived effective sample |

What it tests, what it cannot: timing skill on this one path. It does not test other price paths (L3's paths are returns only),
and it does not test the LP rule itself, as every shifted run keeps the same LP. A shifted strategy holds the same F values, so
the median shifted run is "the same exposure with no timing". Only the columns above move, so a v3.2 fail or pass says nothing
about its EMA100 share rule, and v2.2's joint shift does not separate F from the strength switch.

### Objective assessment

What the numbers show:
- **All four judged rows are under the reference p <= 0.05.** The real alignment beats the median alignment by 26.5 pts (v3.2 on), 20.0 pts (v3.2 off), 39.1 pts (v2.2 `0x4585` on) and 33.8 pts (off).
- **v2.2 `0x4585`: no shift reaches the real run.** p = 1 / 570, the lowest value this grid can give. The best shift is about 19 pts of CAGR below the real run in on mode.
- **v3.2: 7 shifts of 629 reach the real run in on mode, and they are 2 groups, not 7 separate events.** The shifts are 318-330 and 573-576 (off mode: 6 shifts, 321-330 and 573-576). On Calmar, 3 shifts reach it (327, 330, 576) in on mode and 2 (324, 330) in off mode. So about two other alignments on this path match the real one.
- **The same F values at other dates do much worse on v3.2.** The median run loses money and 62% of shifts have CAGR <= 0. This does not isolate timing from exposure: realised exposure was not measured (see the limits below).
- **Rebuild counts do not explain the gap on v2.2.** They are almost equal (211 vs 213). v3.2's shifted runs rebuild more (188 vs 164). The real on-mode run paid $8,050 of swap fees in 5.3 years, so this difference looks small against 26.5 pts of CAGR. The swap fees of the shifted runs were not kept, so this is an estimate. No run nears the ~1,000-rebuild gas flag.

What the numbers cannot show:
- **Not a clean test.** The spec constants were frozen after all this data, and the rules were developed on these paths. A rule shaped on a path can beat its own misaligned copies on that path. The p values are not adjusted for the versions tried (L4: N >= 29 for v3.2, N >= 8 for v2.2).
- **One price path.** L2 says nothing about other paths. `0x7aea`, the v2.2 target pool, has low p values, but it is 2 years, reported only, and shares the signal engine and much of its calendar with `0x4585`.
- **p is a rank among dependent alignments.** 500+ shifts are about 31-97 independent alignments by our rough count. That count uses the first 1/e crossing and may overstate independence, as market regimes last longer than 20-24 days.
- **It does not say which part works.** v2.2 shifts F and the strength switch together. On both strategies, all shifted runs keep the same LP, so a low p says nothing about the LP's own edge (EXP-186 / EXP-187 tested that side for v2).
- **A low p is not timing alone.** A shifted run pairs a moved F with the real range breaks and, on v3.2, the real EMA share. Range-break exits stay on their real dates. So a shifted run is also a book whose parts no longer agree, and p can reward that agreement as well as timing. The theory table's "the same exposure with no timing" is therefore too strong: holding the same F values does not prove the same realised exposure.
- **The null has known defects.** The wrap adds one artificial decision jump and the start state differs (Kalyuzhny 2020). With one jump in 5 years the wrap effect should be small. It was not measured.
- **Fees and fill model as L0.** JIT dilution is not modelled.

How this sits with the other layers: L3 (the mean return's bootstrap) and L4 (Sharpe after N trials) fail on the same runs, and L2 passes. They ask different questions. L2 asks whether these dates beat other dates on this path. L3 and L4 ask whether the return stream is strong enough after sampling noise and the number of tries. A pass here does not cancel those fails.

## L2 shuffle (the fit note's method)

Dino decided on 2026-11-02 to run the method the fit note names, as the circular shift above dropped it on a false premise
(Deviations (L2)). Four parts, as L1 + L5. Both tests are reported; neither replaces the other.

### Method and reference rule (pre-registered 2026-11-02, before any shuffled run)

The fit note: "keep the real pool path, shuffle the dates of F changes / exits (same count and sizes), 500-1,000 reruns, p =
share of shuffles that beat the real rule."

**What moves.** The same columns as the shift, read as one decision per day: v3.2 `Fp` + `diverged`; v2.2 `F` + `strong`. Over the
judged days (start − 1 .. end − 1), a change day is a day on which any of the columns differs from the day before. The shuffle
draws as many change days at random, without replacement, from all judged days except the first, sorts them, and places the
levels on them in their real order (`shuffle_decisions`). So the first level, the order of the levels, the count of changes and
the size of each change stay. The dates and the holding times become random.

**What stays real.** As the shift: minute prices and pool data, fees, range breaks, v3.2's EMA100 ETH share at a build, v3.2's gas
cap, claims, reinvest, costs, all L0 settings. So range-break exits do not move, though the fit note names exits.

**Draws.** 500 per run (`L2_DRAWS`, the low end of the fit note's range, for machine time), each with its own random stream
`default_rng([20261102, draw])`, draw = 1..500. Seed fixed here, before any draw is computed. Runs: v3.2 `0x88e6`, v2.2 `0x4585`,
v2.2 `0x7aea` (reported), same windows as the shift, both reinvest modes. Real run: the L0 run of the same mode.

**Numbers per run and mode.** As the shift (`l2_report`, command `l2shuffle`): CAGR and Calmar, the draws' 5% / median / 95% CAGR and
median Calmar, p = (1 + draws at least as good as the real run) / (1 + draws) for CAGR and Calmar, real and median rebuild count.
The memory columns describe the real decision series only; the "independent alignments" count does not apply to random draws.

**Reference rule (a reference, not the decision).** Passes when p(CAGR) <= 0.05, judged on v3.2 `0x88e6` and v2.2 `0x4585`, both
modes. `0x7aea` is reported, not judged. p(Calmar) is reported beside it.

Written down before the runs: if the shift and the shuffle disagree, both stand as reported, and the assessment says where and
does not pick the convenient one.

The switch changes no number when off: checked against the L0 files before any draw runs. The `l2` shift table is recomputed
after the code change to confirm it does not change.

### Theory

| measure | what it answers | source | limits here |
|---|---|---|---|
| random change dates with the levels in order | could the same sequence of decisions, on random dates, reach this result | Monte Carlo permutation of a rule's outputs against the market (Aronson 2006; Masters 2018, as in L2 above; both shuffle returns, not change dates); this form is the fit note's, ours | holding times change, so a low p can also reflect the real rhythm of decisions (long holds at F = 0 or 1), not only their dates. This differs from the shift, which keeps the rhythm |
| p = (1 + b) / (1 + m) | exact for random draws | Phipson & Smyth (2010), as above | here the draws are really random, so the formula fits better than on the shift's grid. The draws still share one price path |

The limits of L2 above stay: one path, seen before the freeze, no adjustment for trials, F moved against real range breaks.

### Objective assessment

What the numbers show:
- **The two methods give the same reference verdict on the judged runs.** All four judged rows are under p <= 0.05 by the shuffle as by the shift. No draw of 500 reaches the real CAGR or Calmar on v3.2 `0x88e6` or v2.2 `0x4585`: p = 1 / 501, the lowest value 500 draws can give.
- **v3.2: the shuffle's draws sit closer together than the shift's.** The median is +2.6% (on), the best draw is +14.3%, against the real +23.7%. In the shift, a few alignments reached the real run.
- **v2.2 `0x4585`: random dates do well, but not as well as the real ones.** The median draw makes +22.0% and every draw is positive. The best draw (+41.2% on, +41.8% off) comes within 5 pts on and 2 pts off of the real run.
- **On v2.2 the shuffle also changes the exposure.** It keeps the order of the levels but not the time at each level. Mean F over the judged days (computed after review, not pre-registered):

  | run | real mean F | drawn mean F, 5% / median / 95% |
  |---|---|---|
  | v3.2 `0x88e6` (`Fp`) | 0.527 | 0.509 / 0.524 / 0.537 |
  | v2.2 `0x4585` | 0.724 | 0.551 / 0.571 / 0.590 |
  | v2.2 `0x7aea` | 0.687 | 0.562 / 0.588 / 0.613 |

  So v2.2's draws hold less F than the real run, while the shift keeps the real 0.724 exactly. Yet the shuffle's median draw (+22.0%) is well above the shift's (+7.1%). The two nulls differ in exposure and in rhythm, and nothing here separates those effects or says why random dates do this well on v2.2. No baseline (such as a constant F at the real mean) was run.
- **`0x7aea` (reported): 5 and 7 draws reach the real run, p 0.012 / 0.016.**

What the numbers cannot show:
- **The shuffle also breaks the rhythm.** The real decisions hold for up to 94-120 days, and the draws' longest hold has a median of 16-22 days. v3.2's draws also rebuild more (200 vs 164). A low p here can come from the real rhythm (long holds out of or in the market) as well as from the dates. The shift keeps the rhythm and the shuffle does not.
- **Same verdict, different nulls.** The two tests give the same reference verdict on the judged runs, so the verdict does not hang on the choice. Their null distributions differ a lot (v3.2 drawn CAGR 5-95%: shift −17.0..+13.5%, shuffle −4.7..+10.6%; v2.2 `0x4585` median: +7.1% vs +22.0%), and so do their p values (v3.2: 0.013 vs 0.002). Both share one price path and the same real range breaks, so their agreement is not independent evidence.
- **The other limits of L2 stand:**
  - one price path, seen before the freeze;
  - no adjustment for the trials in L4;
  - F moved against real range breaks and, on v3.2, the real EMA share;
  - v2.2's F and strength switch moved together.
- **500 draws** is the low end of the fit note's 500-1,000. With 0 of 500 draws reaching the real run, more draws are expected to lower p, but a later draw could still reach it.

## Alpha / beta: holding, passive LP and constant F

Not a layer of the ladder. Dino asked (2026-11-03) how much of each strategy's return is alpha and how much is beta. A first
regression against holding the coin (computed before this section, numbers below) left an open question: holding alone credits
the LP's passive income to alpha. Dino chose (option 1) to add two baselines run through the same script: a passive LP and a
constant F. Four parts, as L1 + L5.

### Method and reference rule (pre-registered 2026-11-03, before any baseline run)

Question: which part of the return is market exposure, which part is the LP's passive income, which part is the average
deployment, and which part is the timing of F?

**Returns.** Daily simple returns between consecutive 00:00 UTC values of `daily_net_value` (`daily_simple_returns`; v3.2's last,
non-midnight value is dropped). Same runs, windows, units and settings as L0. Both reinvest modes.

**Benchmarks**, each a daily return series on the same days:

1. **Hold** (Binance closes, `held_price`: day D's close at D + 1 00:00). v3.2: ETH/USD (`ethusdt`). v2.2: BTC held in ETH
   (1 / `ethbtc`). For v2.2 also in USD (its net value x `ethusdt`), against ETH and against ETH + BTC (two regressors).
2. **Passive LP**: the same script, with the decision columns held on every judged day (`fix_decisions`, start − 1 .. end − 1).
   - v3.2: `Fp` = 1, `diverged` = 0 (`BENCH_F=1`).
   - v2.2: `F` = 1, `strong` = False (`BENCH_F=1 BENCH_WEAK=1`): the weak side, a ±20% LP with the whole book.
3. **Constant F**: `F` held at the real run's own mean over the judged days (`BENCH_F=mean`).
   - v3.2: `Fp` = its mean, `diverged` = 0.
   - v2.2, two runs: **3a** the strength switch stays real (`BENCH_F=mean`); **3b** it is held weak too (`BENCH_F=mean BENCH_WEAK=1`).

**What stays real in 2 and 3.** Minute prices, pool data, fees, range breaks (both sides rebuild or retreat as in the spec, and
the follow rule rebuilds to the held F the next day), claims, reinvest, costs, v3.2's gas cap and its EMA100 ETH share at a build.
So the EMA100 share rule of v3.2 is inside every baseline and is not measured here.

**Runs.** v3.2 `0x88e6`: 2 baseline runs. v2.2 `0x4585` (judged) and `0x7aea` (reported): 3 each. 8 runs, each with both
reinvest modes, through `l6_runs.py` (L0 switches on). Off-switch check first: the three L0 runs rerun with no `BENCH_*` must give
the L0 files byte for byte.

**Numbers per strategy run and benchmark** (`strategy_layers.py alphabeta`, `factor_row`):
- OLS of the strategy's daily return on the benchmark's, Newey-West standard errors (Bartlett, 10 lags, `NW_LAGS`): alpha =
  365 x intercept with its t, beta with its t, R^2.
- Excess = 365 x mean(strategy − benchmark), beta held at 1, with its Newey-West t. Against benchmark 3 this is the timing gain.
- Up / down-day beta (benchmark return > 0 / < 0).
- Beta and alpha per calendar year with more than 200 days.
- Each baseline run's CAGR, max DD and Calmar (`l0`).

**Reading, fixed here.** Going from benchmark 1 to 2 removes the passive LP income. Going from 2 to 3 removes the average
deployment. What is left over 3 is the timing of F (and on v3.2 of the divergence flag). On v2.2, 3a vs 3b separates the strength
switch from F.

**Reference rule (a reference, not the decision).** Timing is shown when the excess over benchmark 3 (v3.2: 3; v2.2: 3b) is > 0
with Newey-West t >= 2, in both reinvest modes. Judged: v3.2 `0x88e6`, v2.2 `0x4585`; `0x7aea` reported. Harvey & Liu's (2015)
t >= 3 for many tested strategies is reported beside it (L4: N >= 29 for v3.2, N >= 8 for v2.2).

**Numbers already seen before this section** (holding only, reinvest on, the same code): v3.2 beta 0.23 to ETH, alpha +19.7%
(t 2.33); v2.2 beta 0.61 to BTC in ETH, alpha +27.7% (t 2.55); in USD, ETH 0.43 + BTC 0.44, alpha +27.0% (t 2.74).

### Theory

| measure | what it answers | source | limits here |
|---|---|---|---|
| alpha, beta from a regression on a benchmark | how much return is left after the benchmark's exposure | Jensen (1968), *J. Finance* 23(2) | one fixed beta for a strategy whose exposure changes every day: the timing effect goes into alpha |
| Newey-West standard errors | t-statistics when daily returns are autocorrelated or heteroskedastic | Newey & West (1987), *Econometrica* 55(3) | 10 lags is our choice (L8 tested lags 1..10) |
| up / down-day beta | does exposure rise in up markets and fall in down markets (timing) | Henriksson & Merton (1981), *J. Business* 54(4) | an LP's payoff is concave by itself, which lowers the up-day beta without any timing |
| passive LP as a benchmark | the LP's income for holding the position, the part any passive LP earns | LP returns as a short-volatility payoff and its loss to arbitrage, LVR: Milionis, Moallemi, Roughgarden & Zhang (2022), [arXiv:2208.06046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06046) | the passive LP here rebuilds after range breaks with the spec's rules, so it is "the strategy's LP, always on", not a textbook buy-and-hold LP |
| constant exposure as a benchmark | timing skill = return over the same average exposure held still | the fixed-weight benchmark of timing studies, e.g. Sharpe (1975), *Financial Analysts Journal* 31(2) | the mean F is taken from the real run, so the baseline knows the run's average deployment in advance |
| t >= 3 | a higher bar when many strategies were tried | Harvey & Liu (2015), *J. Portfolio Management* 42(1); Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016), *RFS* 29(1) | |

  holds more of the LP when the LP gains. An LP's concave payoff lowers the up-day beta by itself (theory table), but the passive
  LP and the constant F share that payoff, so the gap between them and the strategy is the timing.
- **v2.2 has three parts, all positive on `0x4585`.** Passive LP +7.2% (in ETH: the LP held BTC while ETH/BTC fell), the average
  deployment +3.5 pts (3b +10.7%), the strength switch +7.0 pts (3a +17.7%), and the timing of F the rest (strategy +46.3%).
  The excess over 3b is +29.6% / +25.6% (t 2.53 / 2.43).
- **Both strategies' timing is uneven over the years.** v3.2's is spread over most years (2023 near zero). v2.2's is mostly 2022
  (+101% against 3b); 2024 is negative and 2025 +20% is not significant. This is the same pattern as L5.
- **`0x7aea` shows the same order of parts, smaller.** Strategy +25.0% vs 3b +6.9%, excess +15.6% (t 1.47): positive, not
  significant on 2 years.

What the numbers cannot show:
- **Whether the timing is skill.** The t-values are not adjusted for the trials (L4: N >= 29 / 8), and none reaches t 3. The
  constants were fixed after all this data (as L2), so a rule shaped on this path can time this path.
- **A clean split of the parts.** The order 2 -> 3 -> strategy is one order; another order (timing first) can give other shares.
  The constant-F baseline knows the run's mean F in advance. v3.2's EMA100 share rule stays inside every baseline.
- **Independence from L2.** L2 already showed that these dates beat other dates on this path. The constant-F excess is a
  different null (no timing at all instead of other timing) on the same path, not new evidence from new data.
- Gas is not charged; JIT dilution is not modelled (L0).

## LVR split: rebalancing strategy, fees and loss-versus-rebalancing

Not a layer of the ladder. Dino asked (2026-11-03) to split v3.2 and v2.2 into alpha and beta the way Milionis, Moallemi,
Roughgarden & Zhang (2022) split an LP's P&L. The alpha / beta section above regresses daily returns on benchmarks; its passive
LP baseline is "the strategy's LP, always on" and keeps rebuild losses inside. This section measures the LP leg itself: what is
left of the book's P&L after the rebalancing strategy on the book's own holdings.

### Method and reference rule (pre-registered 2026-11-03, before any full run)

**Data.** `ACCOUNT_LOG=1` makes both scripts write the actuator's minute account status (`save_account_minutes`:
`account_<report>.parquet`, "-acct" in the folder): net value, wallet tokens, the LP's principal amounts
(`base_in_position`, `quote_in_position`), principal value (`liquidity_value`), uncollected fees and each token's price in the
run's unit. The run itself does not change. Off-switch check first: the "-acct" runs' `netvalue_*` / `daily_*` files must equal
L0's byte for byte.

**Split** (`lvr_split`, per minute t, unit and prices as the backtest's own: v3.2 USD with the pool's ETH price; v2.2 ETH with
the pool's BTC/ETH price and the 0x88e6 pool's USDC/ETH price):
- beta_t = sum over tokens k of X_k(t-1) x (P_k(t) - P_k(t-1)), X_k = wallet + LP principal + uncollected fees of k: the
  rebalancing strategy of the paper, applied to the whole book. It holds what the book holds, so it carries the F timing, the
  strength switch, the EMA100 share and every exposure choice.
- hedged_t = dNV_t - beta_t: the paper's delta-hedged P&L.
- On a quiet minute (no wallet balance changes): hedged_t = fee_t - lvr_t exactly, fee_t = d(uncollected value) - uncollected
  base x dP, lvr_t = principal base x dP - d(principal value) (>= 0, the paper's LVR on the backtest's minute path).
- On a minute where a wallet balance changes (a rebuild, swap, claim, reinvest or follow step): exec_t = hedged_t. It holds the
  swap costs, the out-of-range rebuild steps and that minute's own fee - lvr.
- Checks: beta + fee - lvr + exec = dNV to float precision; lvr >= 0 on quiet minutes.

**Numbers per run** (`lvr_report`): each part as 365 x the mean daily share of the day's first net value (so they add up
like the alpha / beta table, not to CAGR); hedged with its Newey-West t (10 lags) on the daily shares; fee / lvr over the
run; the share of quiet minutes; calendar-year fee, lvr, exec and hedged; the hedged sum with the hedge rebuilt every 1 min,
1 h and 1 day (the paper's Table 1 check: coarser hedges leave more market risk inside).

**Runs** (`l6_runs.py`, L0 switches on, both reinvest modes, 3 at a time):
1. The three L0 runs with `ACCOUNT_LOG=1`: v3.2 `0x88e6`, v2.2 `0x4585` (judged), v2.2 `0x7aea` (reported).
2. The passive LP baselines of the alpha / beta section with `ACCOUNT_LOG=1` (v3.2 `BENCH_F=1`; v2.2 `BENCH_F=1 BENCH_WEAK=1`
   on both pools): the LP leg with the whole book, no timing.

**Reference rule (a reference, not the decision).** The LP leg earns when the real run's hedged part is > 0 with Newey-West
t >= 2 in both reinvest modes; it loses when < 0 with t <= -2 in both; otherwise it is not told apart from 0. Judged: v3.2
`0x88e6`, v2.2 `0x4585`; `0x7aea` and the passive baselines reported. fee / lvr > 1 is reported beside it (fees cover the
loss to arbitrage before execution costs).

**Seen before this section** (code smoke test, reinvest on, 2022-01-02..2022-02-28 only): the parts add up to 1.5e-11 USD /
4e-16 ETH, lvr >= 0 on every quiet minute; fee / lvr 0.74 (v3.2 `0x88e6`) and 0.97 (v2.2 `0x4585`) over those two months.

**Theory.**

| measure | what it answers | source | limits here |
|---|---|---|---|
| rebalancing strategy, LVR | the LP's P&L minus a strategy that holds the same assets but trades at market prices = fees - LVR | Milionis, Moallemi, Roughgarden & Zhang (2022), [arXiv:2208.06046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06046), eq. (13) / (24), Table 1 | the backtest's price is the pool's own minute price, so LVR here is the loss along that path at minute steps, not against a CEX; arbitrage inside a minute and JIT are not modelled |
| LVR with fees and discrete blocks | arbitrageurs pay fees and act once per block, so they take only part of the continuous-time LVR | Milionis, Moallemi & Roughgarden (2023), *Automated Market Making and Arbitrage Profits in the Presence of Fees*; Nezlobin & Tassy (2025), [arXiv:2505.05113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05113) | not modelled; minute data already skips the price path inside a minute |
| measured v3 LP losses | on major Uniswap pools arbitrage losses exceeded fees; v3 passive LPs did worse than v2 | Fritsch & Canidio (2024), [arXiv:2404.05803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05803) | other pools, periods and LP sets |
| Newey-West t | a t-value for autocorrelated daily shares | Newey & West (1987), *Econometrica* 55(3) | 10 lags, as the alpha / beta section |
